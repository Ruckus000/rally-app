Rally — Privacy Policy

Effective {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}. Published by {{LEGAL_ENTITY}}. Questions: {{SUPPORT_EMAIL}}.

Rally is a small app for writing down a few goals for the week and sharing them with a circle of people you invite. This page describes what it actually stores, where that goes, and how to get rid of it. It describes the app as built, not as a category of app.

The short version

  • You can use Rally without giving us an email address, a phone number, or your real name. Accounts are created anonymously.
  • We do not run analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting services. There is no tracking SDK in the app, and we do not use the iOS advertising identifier.
  • What we hold is the content you create: a display name, your goals, notes, cheers, weekly totals, and any photos you attach.
  • The text of a goal, and any photo you upload, is sent to Google's Gemini API to be priced and safety-screened. Nothing identifying you goes with it.
  • You can delete your account from inside the app, under Me → Settings. It is hidden from everyone straight away and destroyed fourteen days later, and you can take it back at any point in between.

Your account, and how you are identified

When you tap "Get started", the app creates an anonymous account. You are not asked for an email address or a password, and none is created. What identifies you is a random account identifier (a UUID) generated by our server, plus an automatically derived handle of the form anon_ followed by twelve characters of that identifier. You never choose the handle and no screen offers to change it.

On iPhone you can optionally attach an Apple identity to that account ("Secure this account"), which is what lets you sign back in after reinstalling. When you do:

  • We ask Apple for no name and no email address. The app requests zero scopes. What we receive and keep is an Apple-issued identifier that says "this Apple account belongs to this Rally account".
  • We also keep one Apple-issued token, and it has a single purpose: when your account is deleted we use it to tell Apple this app is finished with your Apple ID, so Rally stops appearing in your Apple account's sign-in list. Apple asks apps to do this and we would rather do it than leave it to you. The token is stored where no signed-in account can read it — not even yours — it is never sent to your phone, and it is destroyed with the account. The only request we ever make with it is that revocation.
  • Your Rally account keeps the same identifier, so nothing you have written has to move.
  • Your interaction with Apple's sign-in sheet is between you and Apple, and Apple's own privacy policy governs it.

Sign-in and session tokens are handled by Supabase, our backend provider, and are refreshed automatically. Android accounts currently have no way to attach an identity, and so no way to be recovered if the app is deleted.

  • A push notification token, if you turn on notifications. This is an Expo push token — a routing address for this install — stored alongside your account identifier and the string ios or android. Automatic counters

To stop one misbehaving client from exhausting a shared quota, we count how many times each account calls our AI features per day. That record is your account identifier, a date, and a number. It contains no goal text.

What we do not collect

Stated as flatly as we can, because this is the part people most often assume the worst about. Rally does not collect, store, or transmit:

  • Your email address or phone number. There is no field for either.
  • Your location, at any precision. There is no location permission and no column that could hold one.
  • Your contacts or address book.
  • The advertising identifier (IDFA), or any other cross-app identifier. The app does not include the App Tracking Transparency framework, because it has nothing to ask you for.
  • Analytics or usage telemetry. There is no analytics SDK of any kind in the app.
  • Crash reports. When a screen fails, the app writes what happened to your own phone so you can read it back; that record is never uploaded. If you want us to see it, you have to send it to us yourself.
  • Payment or financial information. There is nothing to buy.
  • Anything from your camera. Camera access is not requested.

We do not sell your data, we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers, and we do not use it to build a profile of you for any purpose outside the app.

Who can see what

Other people using Rally

Every goal you write carries an audience, which you set:

  • Friends — visible to the members of your circle.
  • Everyone — visible to every signed-in Rally account, along with any screened photo attached to it and any notes written on it. Treat this as public.
  • Private — visible to you and to anyone you have paired with on that goal, and to nobody else.

This is enforced on our server by database policies rather than by the app filtering what it draws, so an audience setting holds even if a client misbehaves.

Beyond your goals, someone in your circle can see your display name, your handle, your profile picture, notes on goals they can see, your pairing progress, your cheers, your weekly totals, and the date you joined the circle. A profile picture, once approved, is visible to every signed-in account, because it is drawn on every screen that names you.

One consequence worth stating plainly: weekly totals are not hidden by blocking. Blocking someone hides what they wrote; it does not remove their numbers from the circle's shared arithmetic, because that would make "how did we do this week" give a different answer to every member.

Automated processing, and what goes to Google

Two features send your content to a third-party model.

Pricing and screening a goal. As you type a goal, the app asks our server what it is worth. Our server sends the goal's title and category to Google's Gemini API — twice: once to assign a point value, and once to decide whether the goal describes something harmful. Nothing identifying you is sent with it: no account identifier, no name, no handle, no device information. Google receives the sentence and nothing else. If the goal appears to describe serious harm, the app declines to save it.

Screening a photo. When you upload a profile picture or a photo on a goal, our server downloads it and sends the image itself to the same Gemini API to check it before anyone else can see it. Again, no identifier travels with it. A picture that fails the check is deleted from our storage outright, along with the record of it, and the person who uploaded it is told the photo cannot be used. Nobody but you ever sees an unscreened image — not even you, in the case of a profile picture, which shows your initials until it has been checked.

Two honest caveats about this arrangement:

  • We use Google's standard developer API and have not negotiated separate data-processing terms with them. We do not send any instruction asking Google not to retain or learn from what we send. What Google does with it is governed by the terms that apply to our API access, not by this page. If that matters to you, do not put anything in a goal you would not want a third party to read, and do not upload a photo you would not want screened.
  • The result of pricing a goal is cached on our servers permanently. The cache is keyed on the goal's text so the same goal is not priced twice, which means the text of your goal is stored indefinitely, in plain text, with no link to your account and no expiry. It cannot be traced back to you and it is not deleted when your account is. It is described again under Deleting your account, because it is the one thing deletion does not reach.

No decision made by these models has a legal effect on you. The worst outcome is a goal you cannot save or a photo you cannot use, and you can email us about either.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, two different things can reach you. A weekly reminder is scheduled by your own phone and never leaves the device. A cheer is a push notification, and reaching you means our server sends your push token, the cheering person's display name, and the title of your goal to Expo, which passes it to Apple or Google for delivery. That text appears on your lock screen. You can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings; when a delivery service tells us a token is no longer valid, we delete it.

What is stored on your phone

Rally works offline, so your week lives on your device as well as on our server. The app stores your goals, circle, notes, notifications, history, totals, and your block list in the device's own app storage, plus a short log of any screen that failed. This data is:

  • Kept out of iCloud backups on iOS, and out of Google Auto Backup, device-to-device transfer, and adb backup on Android;
  • Protected by the operating system's own disk encryption, and not separately encrypted by us;
  • Removed entirely when you delete the app.

One exception, so the paragraph above is not read more widely than it should be: when you attach a photo, the app keeps its own copy of the picked image in its documents folder so the upload survives being interrupted. Unlike the rest, that folder is included in device backups.

How long we keep things

We keep what you have written for as long as your account exists. There is no automatic expiry on goals, notes, cheers, notifications or weekly totals, and no scheduled deletion job — so assume that anything you put into Rally stays there until you or we remove it.

Two things you should know cannot be undone from inside the app:

  • A note cannot be edited or deleted, by you or by the person who received it. It goes away only when the goal it hangs off is deleted, or when one of the accounts involved is deleted.
  • A closed week's totals cannot be changed or removed. A week closes once.

Deleting a goal deletes any photo attached to it; the file itself is swept from storage shortly afterwards. Withdrawing a cheer deletes the notification it created.

Deleting your account and your data

The control is in the app, under Me → Settings → Delete my account. The screen there lists what goes and what stays before you confirm.

It happens in two steps, and the gap between them is deliberate:

  1. Straight away. Your account stops being visible to anybody else. You are removed from your circle's roster and its weekly ranking, and every goal, note, cheer and photo you have written stops being readable — including goals you had set to everyone. Nothing new can be written from the account either. You can still see your own account, because that is what makes the next step reversible.
  2. Fourteen days later. It is destroyed. Your auth record goes, and the database cascade takes everything hanging off it. This cannot be undone by you or by us.

To take it back inside that fortnight, open the app and tap Keep my account on the first screen — that works on any phone, including an account with no sign-in attached. On iPhone, signing back in with Apple also cancels a pending deletion.

We chose a fortnight rather than deleting on the spot for one reason: most Rally accounts are anonymous, and on Android every account is, so nothing but the session on your own phone holds the key to yours. Deleting instantly would make a mistaken tap permanent for the people least able to recover from it.

If you cannot reach the control — the app will not open, or you have already deleted it from your phone — email {{SUPPORT_EMAIL}} with the subject Delete my account, and tell us your display name and your circle's name or invite code. We have no email address on file to match you by. Expect a reply within {{RESPONSE_TIME}}.

Deleting the app from your phone removes everything stored locally, but it does not delete what is on our server. On Android in particular, deleting the app leaves you with no way back into the account — so if you want the account gone, email us before you uninstall.

What deletion removes

Before anything else is removed, we ask Apple to revoke the token described above, so this app stops appearing in your Apple account. If Apple cannot be reached at that moment we go ahead with the deletion anyway rather than delay it — your data going is the more important half, and you can always remove the app yourself from your Apple ID settings.

Your profile and display name, your profile picture, every goal you wrote, every photo attached to them, every note you wrote or received, your cheers, your pairings, your weekly totals, your notifications, your push tokens, your blocks, your reports, your circle memberships, your daily usage counters, and the notifications your cheers left sitting in other people's apps.

What deletion does not remove, and why

  • Goal text held in the pricing cache. As described above, the cache holds goal text with no link to any account. Because nothing records who typed a given line, we cannot find your entries in order to remove them. They contain no identifier of any kind.
  • Notes and cheers other people wrote on your goals go when your goals go. But things you wrote on their goals are deleted too, which means a conversation may lose one side of itself.
  • A circle you created survives you, so that the people still in it are not thrown out of it. Your membership and your name are removed from it.
  • Reports other people filed about you are kept, so that deleting an account cannot erase a safety record. They are readable by nobody through the app.

Reporting and blocking

You can block someone, and you can report a goal, a note or a profile. Both are described on our support page, which is the place to start if something has gone wrong.

Two things about how these work, because both affect other people's privacy:

  • Blocking works in both directions. When you block someone, you stop seeing their content and they stop seeing yours. Nothing in the app tells them they have been blocked — but their cheers stop landing and your name stops appearing for them, and an attentive person can work out what happened. There is no version of a block that stops the contact without being inferable.
  • A report is not readable by the person it is about, or by anyone else using the app. Reports are locked away from every account, including the one that filed them. We built it that way because the one thing someone filing a report is owed is that its subject cannot find it.

Children

Rally is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 13. We do not knowingly collect anything from a child under 13. If you believe a child has created an account, email {{SUPPORT_EMAIL}} and we will delete it.

Security

Access to everything in Rally is controlled at the database level rather than by the app deciding what to show, so a client cannot ask for content it is not entitled to. Photos live in private storage and are reachable only through links that expire after an hour. Server-only records — push tokens, usage counters, the report queue — are readable by no signed-in account at all.

What we do not claim: your content is not end-to-end encrypted, and it is not encrypted at rest beyond what our hosting provider and your phone's operating system do by default. We can read what you write, and so, technically, can our hosting provider. No service can promise perfect security, and we do not.

Where your data is held

Our database and file storage are hosted in Canada. Google's and Expo's services may process data elsewhere, including the United States. If you use Rally from outside those countries, you are asking us to move your data there in order to provide the app.

Depending on where you live — the UK, the EEA, California and several other places have rules like this — you may have the right to see what we hold about you, correct it, delete it, obtain a copy, object to some of what we do with it, or complain to a regulator. We do not require you to have any particular status to exercise these: email {{SUPPORT_EMAIL}} and we will do it. We will not charge you or treat you differently for asking.

Our legal basis, where one is needed, is that we cannot provide the app without processing what you write into it, together with a legitimate interest in keeping the service safe (screening, reporting and blocking). Notifications and photo library access are asked for separately and you can decline or withdraw them at any time in your phone's settings.
